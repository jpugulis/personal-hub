# 2025 Latgale Expedition

## Overview

**Region:** Latgale, Latvia
**Dates:** 7–9 August 2025
**Duration:** 3 riding days
**Route:** Daugavpils area → Daugavas Loki → Aglona → Andrupene → Kaunata → Dziļūns → Stoļerova → Rēzekne
**Expedition type:** Social gravel cycling expedition focused on discovering Latgale using quiet gravel roads, forests and lakes rather than riding on highways.

# Planning Philosophy

Before planning the route, the group agreed on several important principles:

- Prioritize scenic gravel roads.
- Avoid highways whenever possible.
- Avoid muddy MTB singletracks.
- Choose roads suitable for mixed bicycle types.
- Include lakes and swimming opportunities whenever possible.
- The destination is less important than spending quality time together.

Several route ideas were discussed, with the strongest recommendations pointing towards:

- Daugavas Loki
- Rāznas National Park
- Ezertaka
- Sēlija region south of Daugavpils

The final route combined several of these ideas.

---

# Participants

The expedition was planned as a social group ride with mixed experience levels. The planning discussion also included inviting new riders and making the route enjoyable for less experienced participants.

**Toms Ziemelis led morning yoga sessions** on two of the three mornings, using Tadej Pogačar's activation routine to wake up the leg muscles before riding. This is the first appearance in the archive of any deliberate pre-ride preparation, and it is worth recording as a practice that could be repeated.

# Planning Insights

The corrected data supports a different — and more useful — set of conclusions than the original report drew.

## The group starts late, not early

The original report claimed the group "naturally settles into" an 08:00 start. It does not, and never has. Across the whole archive the pattern is consistent:

| Expedition | Average start |
|---|---:|
| 2023 Estonia | 11:46 |
| 2024 Gauja | 11:55 |
| 2025 Latgale | **11:09** |
| 2026 Kurzeme | **10:19** |

The group's natural start is **around 11:00**. This is not a failing, but it must be planned for honestly: a day that begins at 11:00 and includes four hours of stopping will finish after 20:00. Latgale's Day 1 finished at 20:36 and Day 2 at 19:42, both of which the original report concealed by reporting finishes of 17:36 and 16:42.

**Any stage that must finish by a fixed time should be planned from a realistic 11:00 start, not an aspirational 08:00 one** — or the early start should be explicitly agreed with the group the evening before, which is exactly what made the 2026 Kurzeme Day 2 work.

## Latgale is hilly

At 9.6 m/km, Latgale is the **hilliest region the group has ridden** — more than double the 2026 Kurzeme coast at 4.7 m/km, and well above Gauja at 7.4 m/km. The reputation of Gauja as the demanding region is not supported by the gradient data: Gauja was hard because of surface and weather, not climbing.

## Roughly half of every day is spent stopped

At 49.0% stopped, Latgale sits between Gauja (50.5%) and Estonia (44.4%), and below Kurzeme (54.4%). This is a stable characteristic of the group across four years and should be treated as a planning constant rather than a variable: **budget roughly one hour stopped for every hour moving.**

## The short final day works

Day 3 at 36.5 km remains the correct model for a final day, and the group has repeated it since. It leaves comfortable margin for travel home without anyone rushing.

# Lessons for Future Expeditions

## What worked well

- National Parks are excellent expedition destinations.
- Gravel roads create a more enjoyable experience than busy asphalt roads.
- 70–90 km per day is comfortable for mixed groups.
- Scenic stops naturally become part of the day rather than interruptions.
- The final day should remain shorter to simplify travel home.
- Morning yoga before riding was a genuine improvement and should be repeated.
- Beginning and ending the tour by bicycle rather than car keeps the logistics simple.

## Things to remember

- Continue avoiding highways whenever possible.
- Continue balancing gravel quality with rideability.
- Routes should remain accessible to mixed bicycle types.
- Leave plenty of time for cafés, viewpoints and conversations.
- **Convert GPX timestamps from UTC to local time.** This report's original version did not, and the resulting error propagated into the group's planning assumptions for a full year.
- **Plan around an 11:00 start unless an earlier one is explicitly agreed.**
- Latgale's climbing is substantial — do not plan Latgale distances using Kurzeme gradients.

---

# Influence on Future Planning

The 2025 Latgale expedition established several planning principles that continue to guide Baltic Cycling Expeditions:

- Scenic value is more important than maximum distance.
- National Parks consistently provide excellent riding.
- 70–90 km riding days are close to ideal, with ~71 km the actual Latgale average.
- Relaxed schedules create better overall experiences than tightly packed itineraries.
- A short final day timed to transport is the correct way to end a tour — a principle the 2026 Kurzeme expedition adopted directly and executed to the minute.
- Roughly half of each day will be spent stopped. Plan for it rather than being surprised by it.

One further lesson emerged only in 2026, when this report was checked against its source data: **an expedition archive is only as useful as it is accurate.** A single unconverted timezone produced a confident, specific and entirely false planning principle that survived unchallenged for a year. Figures in these reports should be traceable to a named source, and that source is now Strava for all four expeditions.

2026 Tour de Kurzeme

Overview

Region: Kurzeme, Latvia — Baltic coast from Liepāja to Jūrkalne, then inland via Kuldīga and the Abava valley to Tukums Dates: 30 July – 2 August 2026 Duration: 3 riding days (plus a Day 0 transfer ride and a Day 1 evening add-on) Route: Liepāja → Saraiķi → Ziemupe → Pāvilosta → Ulmale → Labrags → Jūrkalne → Ēdole → Kuldīga → Renda → Sabile → Kandava → Jaunmokas → Tukums Expedition type: Social coastal-and-gravel tour combining Baltic coastline, quiet asphalt highway sections and two long Mežtaka forest-trail segments.

This was the group's fastest, flattest and longest three-day expedition to date, and the first one where nothing went meaningfully wrong. The weather was exceptional throughout — up to 37 °C on the final day — the sea was swimmable, the roads were dry, and not a single puncture was recorded across 248 km.

It was also the expedition that most completely fulfilled the group's own definition of success: everyone finished, nobody was injured, the accommodation became part of the story, and the biggest single day the group has ever ridden together felt easy enough that more than half the tour was spent standing still.

Source Notes

This report is based on:

  • five uploaded GPX tracks (Day 0, Day 1, Day 1 add-on, Day 2, Day 3), recorded on Strava;
  • the original Strava activity descriptions written during the tour;
  • Jānis Pūgulis' recollections and corrections;
  • the group's photographic record.

All distance, elevation, timing, heart-rate and temperature figures below are calculated directly from the GPX tracks. Moving and stopped time are estimates derived from GPS movement and may differ slightly from Strava's own values — Strava reports 12:46 moving time and 19.4 km/h average against the 12:37 and 19.7 km/h calculated here. The 248 km total and 28:01 total activity time agree exactly.

Elevation gain in the per-day sections is computed from the GPX with a smoothed elevation profile to remove barometric noise; on terrain this flat, raw GPS elevation would overstate climbing significantly. This yields 1,035 m for the tour against Strava's own 1,116 m.

The cross-year comparison table later in this report uses Strava's figures throughout, because that is the only method available identically for all four expeditions. The two numbers differ by 7%; neither is wrong, but they must not be mixed.

Two firsts for the group are worth recording. This was the first expedition with two complete newcomers on the roster at once, and the first with a woman rider — a scenario the Group Profile had anticipated but never tested. Both are directly relevant to how the tour was designed and why it worked: the route was chosen to be rideable by everyone rather than fast for the strongest, and the results confirm the profile's assumption that a mixed group is not a constraint if the terrain is chosen honestly.

Bicycles and Equipment

The bicycle mix split cleanly in half:

Gravel bikes — Jānis, Toms Ģērmanis, Artūrs Mountain bikes — Toms Ziemelis, Katrīna, Tomāts

The three gravel bikes were, in Jānis' assessment, exactly the right tool for this route: fast on the long asphalt coastal sections and entirely comfortable on the Mežtaka gravel.

Tomāts rode an older mountain bike with 26" wheels and consequently had the hardest tour of anyone. He finished every stage, but the equipment gap was real and measurable — small wheels, rolling resistance and gearing all working against him across 231 km of mostly flat, fast terrain where a gravel bike gives away almost nothing for free.

This is the third expedition in the archive where a single rider's equipment materially changed their experience of the trip — after Dāvis Dreika's rental bicycle in 2023 and Toms Romanovskis' pre-departure crank failure in 2024. The pattern is now well established enough to act on.

Equipment incidents

The tour was almost entirely clean:

  • Zero punctures across all five tracks. This is unprecedented in the archive.
  • A pre-tour scare: on the Thursday morning before departure, Toms Ziemelis found his tyre completely flat on the way to work. Jānis sourced a spare tyre as backup. After pumping, the original tyre held for the entire tour and the spare was never needed.
  • Artūrs' fork bag worked loose and fell off during riding. This was not a maintenance failure — Artūrs checked the bolts every single morning before setting off. The bolts were either over-tightened or too short, and no amount of daily checking would have caught either.
  • Jānis' water shorts fell out of his saddle bag into the disc brake and were completely destroyed. The bike was unharmed.

Planning lesson

The Ziemelis tyre episode is the model outcome: a problem surfaced two days before departure, a fallback was arranged, and the tour started with redundancy in place. The cost of carrying an unused spare tyre is trivial compared to the cost of a tour-stopping failure 40 km from the nearest shop.

The fork bag is the more instructive of the two incidents, and it points somewhere other than where it first appears. Artūrs checked his bolts every morning, and the bag still came off — because the problem was the hardware, not the routine. Bolts that are too short do not engage enough thread to hold under vibration; bolts that are over-tightened fatigue the threads until they let go. Both failure modes look perfectly fine to a morning inspection.

The lesson is therefore not check your bags — it is verify that the bolts are the correct length and correctly torqued before the tour starts. A daily check confirms that nothing has loosened since yesterday; it cannot tell you that the fastener was wrong from the beginning. For future tours: confirm thread engagement when fitting new luggage, use thread-locker rather than extra torque, and carry a few spare bolts in the relevant sizes.

Nothing important was lost this time, but the same failure with a phone, wallet or tool roll would have been a different story.

Transport and Logistics

Outbound — Riga → Liepāja, 30 July

The group travelled to Liepāja by train, consistent with the standing preference for rail over driving.

The train coincided with the Summer Sound festival exodus to Liepāja and was consequently packed with young festival-goers. Police with drug-detection dogs were conducting checks on board. The journey worked, but it was not a relaxed start.

Return — Tukums → Riga, 2 August

The group reached Tukums railway station at 18:00 exactly as planned, and returned on one of the new VIVI trains, described as a genuinely pleasant ride.

Planning lesson

Check the regional event calendar before booking outbound travel. The Liepāja train on the eve of Summer Sound is a predictably difficult journey with bicycles, and an earlier or later departure would have cost nothing. The archive already contains two expeditions (2023, 2024) where bicycle capacity on trains was a problem; this adds a third variable — festival traffic — that is entirely foreseeable.

The return worked flawlessly and confirms a principle already established in Latgale: plan the final day backwards from a specific train, and finish with margin. The 18:00 target was set in advance and hit precisely.

Route and Timing Analysis

Times are Latvian local time (UTC+3). Stopped time includes meals, swims, sightseeing, regrouping and social pauses. "Pauses" counts stationary periods longer than five minutes.

Day 0 — Riga → Liepāja → BB Camping, Beberliņi

Date: 30 July 2026 Distance: 5.5 km Elevation gain: ~8 m Start: 21:54 · Finish: 22:14 Elapsed: 20 min · Moving: 20 min Average moving speed: 16.4 km/h Temperature: 22–26 °C

A short evening transfer from Liepāja station to the first campsite at Beberliņi lake, ridden in warm darkness after a full and unpleasant train. Twenty minutes, no stops, and the tour was underway.

Planning insight

A short ride from the station to the first night's accommodation is an excellent tour opening. It converts a stressful transport day into a proper start, gets the bikes loaded and tested before any real distance, and means Day 1 begins from a campsite rather than a railway platform. At 5.5 km it cost nothing and added a full evening to the trip.

Day 1 — Beberliņi → Karosta → Saraiķi → Pāvilosta → Ulmale → Labrags

Date: 31 July 2026 Surface/type: Mixed — gravel out of Liepāja, then predominantly quiet coastal highway Distance: 67.8 km Elevation gain: ~104 m Start: 11:47 · Finish: 18:57 Elapsed: 7 h 09 min · Moving: 3 h 13 min · Stopped: 3 h 56 min Average moving speed: 21.1 km/h Average heart rate: 124 bpm · Max: 163 bpm Temperature: 22–32 °C Pauses over 5 min: 8, totalling 3 h 47 min

Ride character

The road out of the campsite was good. To the left lay the Karosta Northern Forts, various rest areas and a memorial — the group decided not to stop and pressed on. The gravel section began, and after the first 12 km came the first shop stop at Saraiķi (12:22–12:42), where they met a local gentleman and another cyclist, adjusted the bicycles, and continued.

From there the route ran on the right-hand side of the highway. A light rain shower passed through mid-stage and was waited out under a tree — the only rain of the entire riding portion of the tour.

Pāvilosta was the centrepiece of the day. The group swam, then ate in the garden behind Café Laiva. The GPX records the reality of that stop clearly: a 35-minute pause at 15:09–15:45 immediately followed by 84 minutes at 16:02–17:27 — nearly two hours in Pāvilosta out of a seven-hour day.

Onward on the highway to Ulmale, where the group stopped for 35 minutes (18:01–18:37) at the big-wave secret surf spot — the underwater moraine clay shoals and rock ridges along the Ulmale–Strante cliff section that had been identified during planning as a possible side objective. It made the itinerary and it earned its stop.

The day finished at Kempings Sīļi in Labrags.

Planning insight

Day 1 is the model of what the group has been trying to build for four years: 68 km, 104 m of climbing, 21.1 km/h moving average, and 55% of the day spent stopped. An average heart rate of 124 bpm across a stage with two swims and a two-hour lunch is not a training ride — it is a day out that happens to cover 68 km.

The decision not to stop at the Karosta forts is worth noting as much as the decision to stop at Pāvilosta. With 3 h 56 min of stopping to allocate, choosing where not to spend it is what made the two-hour Pāvilosta break possible.

Day 1 add-on — Sīļi → Jūrkalne → Virši → Sīļi

Date: 31 July 2026 Distance: 11.4 km Elevation gain: ~20 m Start: 19:28 · Finish: 20:34 Elapsed: 1 h 05 min · Moving: 28 min Average moving speed: 24.4 km/h · Max: 39.3 km/h Average heart rate: 127 bpm Temperature: 20–24 °C

An evening supply run from Kempings Sīļi to the Jūrkalne shop and Virši and back, with several sprint stages thrown in. At 24.4 km/h moving average — the fastest of the whole tour — and a 39.3 km/h maximum, this was clearly ridden for fun rather than logistics.

Planning insight

Unloaded evening rides are a genuinely good idea and should be planned for. With bags left at camp, 11 km takes half an hour, restocks the group for the morning, and gives the stronger riders somewhere to put energy that would otherwise turn into pressure on the main stage pace. It cost the group nothing and produced the highest average speed of the expedition.

Day 2 — Labrags/Sīļi → Jūrkalne → Ēdole → Kuldīga → Renda → Sabile

Date: 1 August 2026 Surface/type: Coastal highway to Kuldīga, then two Mežtaka forest-trail segments Distance: 106.3 km — the longest group day in the archive Elevation gain: ~488 m Start: 09:05 · Finish: 20:34 Elapsed: 11 h 28 min · Moving: 5 h 24 min · Stopped: 6 h 03 min Average moving speed: 19.6 km/h Average heart rate: 114 bpm · Max: 180 bpm Temperature: 19–33 °C Pauses over 5 min: 13, totalling 5 h 30 min

Ride character

An early rise and a 09:05 departure — the earliest start of the tour, and deliberately so. The group rode the short distance to Virši at Jūrkalne for breakfast, recorded as a 40-minute stop at 09:36–10:16.

The road to Kuldīga followed the highway. The stop at Ēdole became one of the quiet highlights of the expedition: the group spent time on a small boardwalk among swans, resting, sunbathing and simply enjoying the day and the surroundings. The GPX logs 38 minutes stationary at 11:24–12:02, though the felt duration was closer to an hour once the approach and departure are included.

Kuldīga was the big lunch break — 69 minutes at 13:17–14:32 at Pagrabiņš, plus bakery, plus a further 15 and 10 minutes either side. Just over an hour and a half in town in total. This is also where the tour's defining decision was made (see below).

After Kuldīga came the two Mežtaka forest sections: Kuldīga – Renda and Renda – Sabile. Both proved comfortably rideable on the group's bicycles. The GPX shows the pace and stop pattern changing character here — three pauses clustered between 16:27 and 18:11 in the Renda area, then two more approaching Sabile.

The group arrived in Sabile at 20:34 and swam in the Abava, making it in just before the rain started. In the evening they walked into town; it was raining, everything was closed, and the town was silent.

Why 106 km

This distance was planned, not accidental. The tour had to reach the Tukums railway line, and Jānis did not want to leave more than 60–70 km for the final day. That required an overnight stop somewhere after Renda and before Jaunmokas. Sabile fit perfectly.

Critically, the group was prepared for this day from the first evening. It was flagged in advance, the early start was agreed, and everyone knew what was coming. It worked.

Planning insight

Day 2 is the most important data point the group has produced since 2024, and it says something specific:

A 106 km day is achievable for a mixed six-rider group with two newcomers and a 26" mountain bike — if the terrain is flat, the surface is good, the start is early, and the group has been told in advance.

The supporting numbers matter more than the headline. Average heart rate was 114 bpm — the lowest of the entire tour, on the longest day. Only 5 h 24 min of the 11 h 28 min was spent moving. The 488 m of climbing across 106 km is 4.6 m/km; the Latgale expedition averaged 11.2 m/km.

The direct comparison is unavoidable: 2024's Day 3 in Gauja was 54.3 km and took 9 h 32 min at 13.8 km/h. 2026's Day 2 was 106.3 km — nearly double — and took under two hours longer. Terrain and surface, not distance, determine what a day costs. This is the same lesson the Gauja report drew, now demonstrated from the opposite direction.

The one genuine caution: an 11.5-hour day ending at 20:34 leaves no margin. It worked because the weather was perfect and nothing broke. In rain, or with one mechanical, this stage would have finished in the dark.

Day 3 — Sabile → Kandava → Jaunmokas → Tukums

Date: 2 August 2026 Surface/type: Mežtaka forest trail, hilly, with substantial gravel Distance: 57.1 km Elevation gain: ~415 m Start: 10:04 · Finish: 18:02 Elapsed: 7 h 57 min · Moving: 3 h 11 min · Stopped: 4 h 46 min Average moving speed: 17.9 km/h Average heart rate: 118 bpm · Max: 167 bpm Temperature: 23–37 °C — the hottest conditions of the tour Pauses over 5 min: 13, totalling 4 h 04 min

Ride character

The day began earlier than the bicycles did. At 07:55 Jānis went out for a 5 km run around Sabile — the puppet park, Vīna kalns and the town centre — finishing with a morning swim in the Abava. Having been woken early, he reasoned that an easy short day on the bike was ahead anyway.

The group's own start was unhurried: a shop visit in Sabile and a geocache hunt — two stops totalling 18 minutes before anyone had covered half a kilometre.

The Sabile – Jaunmokas Mežtaka section began: noticeably hilly with a lot of gravel, but comfortably rideable throughout. Along the way the group met German tourists and had a cheerful conversation.

Kandava became the centre of the day — see below. The group was stationary in and around Kandava from roughly 12:53 to 15:02, a little over two hours.

The second Mežtaka section, Jaunmokas – Tukums, was substantially shortened. It too was hilly with a lot of gravel. In 37 °C, after two hours of jumping and lunch, cutting it was the correct call.

The group reached Tukums railway station at 18:00, exactly as planned. The tour closed as it had opened, with a bicycle: a 7.1 km evening ride home from the Riga end at 19:32.

Planning insight

57 km with 415 m of climbing was the right length for a final day, and the willingness to shorten the second Mežtaka section on the day is the single best tactical decision of the tour. It preserved both the train connection and the group's mood in extreme heat.

Note the climbing distribution: Day 3 packed 415 m into 57 km (7.3 m/km) against Day 2's 4.6 m/km. The final day was the steepest of the expedition by a wide margin — a reminder that the Abava valley and the Tukums approach are not the flat Kurzeme of the coast.

37 °C is a genuine planning constraint, not a detail. The group handled it by riding short, stopping long, and swimming — but a longer final stage in those conditions would have been a different and worse day.

The Kuldīga Decision

The most instructive episode of the expedition happened sitting down, in a restaurant, at kilometre 47 of Day 2.

The Sabile accommodation had been deliberately left unbooked. The plan was to reach Kuldīga at roughly the half-day point and only then decide how far the group could realistically get, and book accordingly.

By the time the group was eating at Pagrabiņš, the mood was mixed. Katrīna was really low. Tomāts was tired and was actively considering options to head back to Liepāja for the Summer Sound party. The 69-minute lunch stop the GPX records was not simply a long meal.

Contrary to what might be expected, the anxiety was about the route rather than the accommodation. The question was not where will we sleep but how far can we actually go.

What resolved it was the group working the problem together. Artūrs and Katrīna pulled up option after option for Jānis to check, systematically, until they came back around to one they had already seen — Viesu māja "Rambules" in the centre of Sabile — which fit the requirement perfectly.

The booking itself needed care. The host had previously stepped back from the guest-house business and had made clear he did not want parties, boat groups or young people looking for a night out. Jānis called and made a deliberate effort to be very polite. The host agreed: he would take them, if they were six people.

They were six people. It worked, and Rambules turned out to be the best accommodation of the tour.

Planning insight

This episode validates a planning method the group has not previously used explicitly: deliberately deferring the final booking to a mid-route decision point.

The conditions that made it work are worth stating precisely, because they will not always hold:

  • The decision point (Kuldīga) was a real town with food, phone signal and multiple onward options.
  • It fell at roughly the half-way point of the day, early enough that the decision still had range.
  • The group participated in solving it rather than waiting for the organiser — which is also what pulled two struggling riders back into the trip.
  • There were genuine alternatives available in the corridor.

It also shows something about group management. Katrīna and Tomāts were both at a low point, and the thing that fixed it was giving them a problem to solve rather than a pep talk. Artūrs and Katrīna searching options turned passengers into participants at exactly the moment it was needed.

A polite phone call is a logistics tool. The host had explicitly disengaged from the business and had good reasons to say no. He said yes to six adults who asked properly. This is worth remembering for every future last-minute booking in a small town.

The Kandava Jump

The best thirty minutes of the expedition were not on a bicycle.

The Day 3 lunch stop landed next to the Kandava sports centre, where the big water jumps are. The group decided to jump.

The allocation was settled by rock–paper–scissors:

  • Jānis won immediately and took the 5 m low jump.
  • Toms Ģērmanis drew the 7 m middle platform.
  • Toms Ziemelis drew the 10 m high jump.

All three went up, and all three jumped. It was, in Jānis' words, scary as hell going up there — but everyone did it. Ziemis flew in with an enormous double-footed jump. Tomy went with a running jump. Jānis went with a flappy-bird.

The GPX records the whole Kandava sequence as a little over two hours stationary between 12:53 and 15:02. It was an amazing stop and a dose of adrenaline before lunch.

Planning insight

The archive keeps producing the same finding from different directions: the defining moments of these expeditions are almost never the planned attractions. The lightning over Võrtsjärv in 2023, the Kuldīga decision and the Kandava jump in 2026 — none were on any itinerary.

What planning can do is leave enough room in the day for them to happen. A tour that budgets 4 h 46 min of stopping into a 57 km day can absorb a two-hour water-jumping detour without threatening the train. A tightly scheduled day cannot.

Deliberately routing near swimming water in summer is a high-return, low-cost planning decision. This tour swam in Pāvilosta, in the Abava at Sabile, and jumped at Kandava. In a heatwave reaching 37 °C, water was not a bonus — it was what made the tour comfortable.

Rock–paper–scissors sent Ziemis to the top board. He went up, and he jumped — a huge double-footed entry. Tomy took 7 m with a running jump; Jānis, having won the draw and taken 5 m, went in flappy-bird style. Nobody backed out.

The Kuldīga phone call

Six tired cyclists in Pagrabiņš, no bed booked for the night, Katrīna low and Tomāts thinking about a train back to Summer Sound — resolved by a group option-search and one very polite phone call to a host who had left the business.

The Ēdole boardwalk and the swans

Nearly forty minutes on a small boardwalk among swans, in the middle of a 106 km day, doing nothing in particular. One of the quietest and best moments of the tour.

Two hours in Pāvilosta

Swim, then the garden behind Café Laiva, then more of both. Almost two hours of a seven-hour day spent in one small coastal town — and the correct decision.

The Ulmale secret surf spot

The big-wave spot along the Ulmale–Strante cliffs went from a planning curiosity to a 35-minute stop on the actual route.

The Summer Sound train

Departure by train packed with festival-goers, with police and drug dogs working the carriages. A memorable start, though not a restful one.

Silence in Sabile

Arrival at 20:34, a swim in the Abava beaten in just before the rain, then an evening walk through a town where the rain had closed everything and nothing at all was open.

The destroyed water shorts

Jānis' water shorts fell from his saddle bag directly into the disc brake and were annihilated. The bike was fine.

Lessons for Future Expeditions

What worked well

  • Flat coastal Kurzeme is ideal terrain for a mixed group. 4.2 m/km of climbing allowed record distance at record-low heart rates.
  • The Day 0 station-to-campsite transfer turned a difficult travel day into a proper tour start for 5.5 km of effort.
  • Announcing the long day from the first evening. Day 2's 106 km worked because nobody was ambushed by it.
  • Deferring the Sabile booking to Kuldīga produced the best accommodation of the tour and let the route be decided on real information.
  • Involving the group in solving the accommodation problem re-engaged two riders who were struggling.
  • Routing through swimmable water — Pāvilosta, the Abava, Kandava — was what made a 37 °C heatwave enjoyable rather than survivable.
  • Shortening the final Mežtaka section on the day, protecting both the 18:00 train and the group's mood.
  • The unloaded evening add-on ride gave the fast riders an outlet without pressuring the main stage.
  • The Mežtaka forest sections were comfortably rideable throughout — a genuine finding, and a strong contrast with the 2024 Gauja MTB experience.
  • Carrying a spare tyre after the Thursday scare. Never used, cost nothing, correct decision.

Things to remember

  • Check for regional festivals before booking outbound trains. Summer Sound made the Liepāja journey unnecessarily difficult and was entirely foreseeable.
  • 26" wheels on an older mountain bike make a fast flat tour meaningfully harder. Tomāts finished everything, but the group should offer newcomers equipment guidance — or a loan — before departure. This is now the third expedition affected by one rider's bicycle.
  • Verify bolt length and torque when fitting luggage — a daily check cannot catch the wrong fastener. Artūrs inspected his fork bag bolts every morning and the bag still fell off, because the bolts were either too short or over-tightened. Use thread-locker instead of extra torque, and carry spare bolts.
  • An 11.5-hour day finishing at 20:34 has no margin. It worked in perfect weather with zero mechanicals. Plan the long stage earlier in the day, or accept that rain converts it into a night finish.
  • Heat above 35 °C is a route constraint. Shorten stages, route to water, and keep the willingness to cut sections on the day.
  • The Abava valley and the Tukums approach are hilly. Do not plan Kurzeme distances on the assumption that Kurzeme is flat — the coast is, the interior is not.
  • A polite, well-made phone call opens doors that an online booking form cannot. Especially with small hosts who have partly left the business.

Points of tension with the standing preferences

Two documented preferences were not followed on this tour, and both deserve an honest note rather than quiet revision:

  1. The Cycling Preferences document states "Hotels and guest houses only. No camping equipment." Two of the three nights were campsites, and the tour carried camping gear. It worked, and BB Camping and Sīļi were both fine — but the preference should either be updated to reflect actual practice or consciously reasserted for the next tour.

  2. The ideal daily distance is documented as 70–90 km, with ~80 km optimal. Day 2 was 106 km. It succeeded, but it succeeded under specific conditions — flat, dry, early start, forewarned group. It should be recorded as a proven ceiling under favourable conditions, not as the new normal. The main-stage average of 77.1 km sits exactly in the documented range and is the better number to plan from.

Influence on Future Planning

The 2026 Kurzeme expedition establishes several principles for the tours that follow:

  • Terrain and surface, not distance, determine what a day costs. 106 km of flat Kurzeme coast was easier than 54 km of wet Gauja MTB. Every future stage should be assessed on gradient and surface first, with distance third.
  • The group's real capacity is higher than previously assumed — under the right conditions. A mixed group with two newcomers rode 106 km at 114 bpm. The constraint was never fitness; it was terrain, weather and equipment.
  • Deferring one booking to a mid-route decision point is a legitimate planning tool. It requires a decision town with food and signal at roughly the half-day mark, and genuine alternatives ahead. Under those conditions it produces better outcomes than booking blind.
  • Plan the final day backwards from a specific train. Latgale established this, and Kurzeme confirmed it by hitting an 18:00 target exactly.
  • Budget stopping time explicitly. 55% of this tour was spent stationary, and that is where all the memorable moments occurred. Stopping time is not slack in the schedule — it is the schedule.
  • Route to water in summer. Swimming was the single highest-value feature of this route and cost nothing in distance.
  • Newcomers change nothing about route design if the terrain is chosen honestly — but they do warrant a pre-departure equipment conversation.
  • The Mežtaka forest-trail network in Kurzeme is suitable for mixed groups. Kuldīga–Renda, Renda–Sabile and Sabile–Jaunmokas were all rideable on gravel and mountain bikes alike. This is a resource worth using again, and a useful correction to the group's post-2024 caution about anything labelled a trail.

Overall Assessment

The 2026 Tour de Kurzeme was the most successful expedition in the archive.

It is the first tour where every rider who started finished the full route. It recorded no punctures, no mechanicals that mattered, and no injuries. It produced the group's longest-ever single day and its lowest heart rates on the same afternoon. It spent more of its total duration stopped — swimming, eating, talking, jumping off a 10-metre platform — than any tour before it, and covered more ground in three days than any tour before it.

Measured against the group's own definition of success, it scores on every line: everyone finished smiling, nobody was rushed, the route created memorable moments, Rambules became part of the experience, and the group was discussing the next tour before this one ended.

The lesson to carry forward is not "ride 106 km days." It is that when the terrain is chosen honestly for the group that is actually riding, the group turns out to be capable of far more than the plan assumed — and has far more fun doing less of the riding.

Kurzeme gave the group flat roads, good gravel, a warm sea and exceptional weather. What the group did with them was stop constantly, swim at every opportunity, solve one real problem together, and get to Tukums at 18:00 exactly as planned.

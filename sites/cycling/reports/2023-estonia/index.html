2023 Estonia – Võrtsjärv Expedition

Overview

Region: Southern Estonia and northern Latvia
Dates: 17–20 August 2023
Duration: 4 riding days
Core route: Valka → Võrtsjärv → local rides around Võrtsjärv → Valka
Extended final route: Valka → Riga, completed by Jānis Pūgulis, Reinis Tihovs and Toms Romanovskis
Expedition type: Social gravel tour built around quiet Estonian roads, lake scenery, shared evenings and an optional ultra-distance ride home.

This became one of the group's defining early expeditions. It combined relaxed social riding around Võrtsjärv with several stories that remained memorable years later: Dāvis Dreika completing the tour on a barely functional rental bicycle, a very heavy first-night celebration, the final-night thunderstorm visibly approaching across the lake, the failed attempt to sleep outside in horizontal rain, and the spontaneous decision by three riders to continue from Valka all the way to Riga by bicycle.

Correction notice — August 2026

Distance and elevation figures in this report have been reconciled against Strava, which now serves as the single source for all four expedition reports so that cross-year comparisons use one consistent method. The original figures were derived from GPX with a different elevation algorithm and ran slightly low.

This report also omitted a Day 0 transfer ride: a 9.7 km night ride on 16 August 2023, starting 22:42, which brought the group from the station to the first night's accommodation. It is the earliest instance of a pattern the group repeated in 2025 and 2026 — beginning the tour with a short evening ride rather than a car. Total distance including it is 389.5 km.

Ride times in this report were checked and found to be correct — they were already in Latvian local time. (The 2025 Latgale report was not, and has been separately corrected.)

Planning and Departure

The WhatsApp group was created on 16 August 2023, one day before departure. Jānis confirmed that the group would meet at Riga Central Station at 10:30 and expected seven participants.

The planning chat shows a practical but informal preparation style:

  • the first-day GPX was shared in advance;
  • train tickets were coordinated because the train was filling quickly;
  • riders agreed to meet in the first carriage;
  • the group discussed chain tools, knives, mosquito repellent, hammocks, tents and bicycle luggage;
  • some riders packed with bike bags, while others planned to use backpacks;
  • everyone was reminded to bring identification because the trip crossed into Estonia;
  • hot weather was expected, but rain and thunderstorms were already considered possible.

The group also knew that the first gravel section could be harder for riders with less suitable bicycles. Jānis described it as necessary “gravel therapy” rather than a reason to avoid the route.

Planning lessons

  • Buy train tickets early when travelling with several bicycles.
  • Agree on a carriage and meeting point before departure.
  • Share GPX tracks in advance.
  • Confirm that at least two riders carry essential repair tools.
  • Check identification requirements for cross-border trips.
  • For mixed camping systems, clarify who is using cabins, tents or hammocks before departure.

Dāvis Dreika and the Rental Bicycle

Dāvis became one of the expedition's lasting legends because he completed the tour on a poor-quality rented bicycle that was extremely inefficient and difficult to ride.

Later group recollections describe it as an “īres miskaste” whose:

  • wheels barely rolled;
  • suspension was rusted solid and did not function;
  • overall condition made every kilometre unnecessarily hard.

Despite this disadvantage, Dāvis continued riding and later became the example used to reassure newcomers that they did not need a perfect gravel bicycle to participate.

Planning lesson

Determination and group support can compensate for imperfect equipment, but future tours should include a basic pre-tour bicycle inspection. At minimum, check:

  • wheels and hubs;
  • brakes;
  • drivetrain;
  • tyres;
  • suspension, if fitted;
  • luggage attachment points.

A badly maintained rental can turn a social ride into unnecessary physical punishment and slow the entire group.

Ride character

The tour began with a manageable afternoon stage from Valka toward Võrtsjärv. The distance and flat terrain were appropriate after travelling by train, although Dāvis' rental bicycle made the stage significantly harder for him than the route statistics suggest.

The first-day timing demonstrates that 60–70 km can work well after a late start when terrain is relatively flat and the overnight location is fixed.

First-night celebration

The first evening became a heavy group celebration and one of the defining social memories of the trip. The group drank considerably, and the evening became an event in its own right rather than merely recovery after Day 1.

The effects are visible in the following day's late start, short distance and extensive stopping time.

Planning insight

A major first-night party should be treated as part of the next day's workload. The following day should intentionally include:

  • a late start;
  • short distance;
  • flat terrain;
  • flexible timing;
  • no critical transport deadline.

Planning insight

The Final Night at Võrtsjärv

The final evening became one of the strongest shared memories of the expedition.

The group had rented a small cabin at the campsite, but some riders—especially Toms Romanovskis and Mikus Geidāns—initially still wanted to sleep outside using hammocks and tarps.

The evening was spent with drinks near the lighthouse or pier, watching a thunderstorm approach from the direction of Latvia. The group followed lightning radar on their phones and could see a dense field of lightning moving steadily closer. At the same time, they watched the flashes in real time across the open lake.

The anticipation lasted for a long time: the storm was visible in the distance, the radar showed it crossing toward them, and the group waited to see whether it would actually reach the campsite.

Eventually it did.

Strong wind and heavy rain arrived, with the rain becoming nearly horizontal. The outdoor sleeping plan rapidly became less attractive. At least one hammock-and-tarp setup failed to provide proper protection, and the rider eventually abandoned the outside arrangement and moved into the cabin after getting wet.

The chat later preserved the place as “the pier where the lightning was awaited.” Jānis also reported being badly bitten by mosquitoes during that final night and still itching after returning home.

This evening became expedition folklore not because of any planned attraction, but because of the combination of:

  • a small lakeside campsite;
  • a shared cabin;
  • riders stubbornly attempting to sleep outside;
  • drinks near the lighthouse;
  • live lightning radar;
  • visible flashes crossing the lake;
  • the eventual arrival of wind and horizontal rain;
  • everyone retreating to safety while laughing at the failed hammock plan.

Planning insight

Accommodation beside open water can become part of the adventure itself. Shared spaces, a cabin fallback and the possibility to remain together during bad weather are more valuable than luxury.

For future tours involving hammocks or tarps:

  • verify the weatherproof setup before departure;
  • ensure the tarp is wide enough for wind-driven rain;
  • keep sleeping bags and dry clothing protected;
  • have an indoor fallback when thunderstorms are forecast;
  • avoid exposed lakeside sleeping during active lightning.

Stage 1 — Võrtsjärv → Valka

The group reached central Valka after approximately:

  • 49.6 km of riding;
  • arrival at roughly 13:37 local time.

This was the natural end of the planned expedition for most participants.

Stage 2 — Valka → Riga

Instead of ending in Valka, Jānis Pūgulis, Reinis Tihovs and Toms Romanovskis decided to continue home by bicycle.

The continuation added approximately:

  • 163 km after Valka;
  • roughly eight further hours of recorded travel;
  • an ultra-distance finish after three preceding tour days.

The ride was dynamic rather than tightly grouped. The chat records that Toms Romanovskis moved ahead alone from around Ieriķi. Other group members following remotely reacted to the progress through Valmiera, Cēsis and toward Riga.

The recorded GPX ends at approximately 21:37, but Jānis later reported reaching home at around 01:00 after the complete return logistics. Toms Romanovskis reported arriving at Uģis' place at 22:18.

The final push transformed a relaxed lake tour into an epic endurance finale. The 26.2 km/h estimated moving average confirms that this was no longer ordinary mixed-group touring; it was a committed road effort by three motivated riders.

Physical aftermath

The post-tour chat records significant accumulated fatigue:

  • Jānis woke during the night with leg cramps;
  • his thighs remained painful for several days;
  • saddle soreness and mosquito bites were still noticeable after returning;
  • Toms Romanovskis reported having almost no energy the next day;
  • Reinis felt comparatively good, cleaned and serviced his bicycle immediately and was already ready for the next ride.

Planning insight

An optional endurance extension can create a legendary finish without forcing the entire group to undertake it. The key is to establish a clear transport exit point—Valka in this case—where the standard expedition can safely end.

For future tours:

  • label extreme extensions as optional;
  • confirm which riders are continuing before the exit point;
  • ensure suitable bicycles, lights, nutrition, charging and repair supplies;
  • preserve a reliable train or vehicle fallback;
  • expect substantial recovery needs afterward;
  • never treat the 213 km day as evidence of the whole group's normal touring capacity.

The expedition had two completely different modes:

  1. Days 1–3: relaxed, social gravel touring with extensive stops and moderate distances.
  2. Day 4 after Valka: a fast, endurance-focused road ride completed by only three riders.

These should never be merged into a single definition of the group's ideal daily distance.

Expedition Legends and Main Memories

Dāvis' rental bicycle

Dāvis completing the trip on a bicycle whose wheels barely rolled and whose suspension was rusted solid became the expedition's first great running joke and an example of determination overcoming poor equipment.

The first-night party

The heavy first evening shaped the rhythm of the whole tour and proved that social evenings are not separate from expedition planning—they directly affect the following day.

The lightning over Võrtsjärv

The group drank beside the lighthouse or pier while watching lightning radar and the storm visibly move from Latvia across the lake. The atmosphere came from waiting, watching and knowing the storm was approaching before it finally arrived.

Hammocks versus horizontal rain

Toms Romanovskis and Mikus Geidāns initially wanted to remain outside despite the rented cabin. Wind-driven rain eventually exposed the limits of at least one tarp setup and forced a retreat indoors.

The 213 km final push

Jānis, Reinis and Toms Romanovskis turned the return from Valka into a spontaneous ultra-distance ride toward Riga, adding a completely unexpected ending to the first expedition.

The group's reaction afterward

The trip ended with riders thanking Jānis for organizing it, praising the company and already discussing future rides and gravel bicycles. Reinis explicitly said he was waiting impatiently for the next event and needed to start saving for a gravel bike.

Costs and Accommodation Notes

The post-tour settlement indicates a simple, relatively inexpensive campsite arrangement:

  • several participants paid approximately €35 for the campsite stay;
  • some who used less accommodation paid around €7;
  • shared drinks and hammock use were settled separately;
  • one accommodation or breakfast-related comment described the value as acceptable for €7—not exceptional, but good enough for the price.

The exact room allocation cannot be reconstructed completely, but the messages confirm a mix of cabin, hammock and camping arrangements.

Planning lesson

Mixed accommodation can keep costs low, but it complicates planning and weather protection. For future group trips, one shared guesthouse or cabin is simpler, especially when storms are possible.

Lessons for Future Expeditions

What worked well

  • One base near Võrtsjärv allowed multiple local loops and relaxed evenings.
  • Southern Estonia offered quiet, flat gravel roads suitable for social riding.
  • Moderate 40–65 km stages left substantial time for long stops and group activities.
  • The lake setting gave the expedition a strong identity.
  • A short recovery day worked well after a heavy first evening.
  • Valka provided an excellent transport and decision point between the standard return and an optional endurance extension.
  • The group atmosphere was strong enough that equipment problems became part of the story rather than ruining the trip.
  • Advance coordination of train carriage, tickets, repair tools and luggage helped seven riders travel together.

Things to remember

  • Inspect rental bicycles before departure.
  • Do not assume a flat route feels easy on badly maintained equipment.
  • Plan a shorter and later-starting day after a major social evening.
  • Lakeside accommodation can contribute more to the experience than luxury accommodation away from nature.
  • Preserve time for long unplanned stops; the GPX proves they were central to this tour.
  • Separate the standard group itinerary from optional high-performance extensions.
  • Keep an indoor fallback for hammocks and tents during thunderstorms.
  • Prepare for mosquitoes near lakes and wetlands.
  • Very long final pushes require lights, food, charging, clothing, repair supplies and a fallback plan.
  • Build recovery time after the tour; the group experienced cramps, muscular fatigue and saddle soreness for several days.

Influence on Future Planning

The 2023 Võrtsjärv expedition established several principles that remained important in later cycling tours:

  • The group experience matters more than daily distance. Days 2 and 3 were short but occupied almost six hours because the stops, food and surroundings were part of the tour.
  • An overnight location can define the expedition. Võrtsjärv was not merely a point on the map; the lake, lighthouse, cabin and storm gave the trip its identity.
  • Shared accommodation improves the social experience. A place where everyone can spend the evening together is almost as important as the route.
  • Mixed equipment should be taken seriously. Dāvis proved that determination can overcome a poor bicycle, but avoidable mechanical disadvantages should be removed before departure.
  • The day after a party must be planned honestly. A flat 42 km loop with a late start was appropriate after the first-night celebration.
  • Optional challenges are better than compulsory suffering. Most riders could finish in Valka while three riders chose the 213 km endurance finale.
  • Weather can become the memory rather than the problem. The storm was manageable because the group had a cabin fallback and could experience it together safely.
  • Epic memories often come from spontaneous decisions. The storm-watching evening and Valka–Riga ride were not checklist attractions, yet they became the defining stories of the expedition.

The strongest lesson from Võrtsjärv is that a successful tour does not require maximum distance every day. It needs the right people, a memorable natural setting, enough time for unpredictable moments, honest recovery days and the freedom for part of the group to turn the final stage into something extraordinary.

# 2024 Gauja National Park Expedition

## Overview

**Region:** Northern Vidzeme and Gauja National Park, Latvia  
**Dates:** 1–4 August 2024  
**Duration:** 4 riding days  
**Route:** Lugaži/Valka → Turna → Strenči → Valmiera → Cēsis → Līgatne → Sigulda → Saulkrasti → Carnikava  
**Expedition type:** Social mixed-surface cycling tour with a high proportion of MTB trails, forest roads, gravel and off-road sections.

This became one of the group's hardest tours. The difficulty came not from extreme daily distance, but from wet conditions, technical MTB terrain, repeated stops, mechanical issues, falls and the cumulative fatigue of four consecutive days.

- Jānis Pūgulis
- Toms Ģērmanis
- Toms Romanovskis
- Reinis Tihovs
- Artūrs Cekuliņš
- Artis Jansons
- Toms Ziemelis

Participation varied during the expedition:

- Artis Jansons joined for the first two days and planned to finish in Cēsis.
- After Toms Romanovskis' crash on Day 2, he stopped riding and returned toward Riga the following day.
- The group gradually split as fatigue and previous commitments accumulated.
- Only **Jānis Pūgulis, Reinis Tihovs and Toms Ģērmanis** remained for the final night and final-day push to Carnikava.

This showed that a route can be technically possible for the strongest riders while still being too demanding for the whole group over several consecutive days.

## Logistics and Accommodation

### Outbound transport

The group used the **11:00 Riga–Valga train** on 1 August 2024.

The original plan was to ride from farther north, but tickets were ultimately bought to **Lugaži** and Day 1 was shortened. The chat also records difficulty finding bicycle space on the train; the group eventually found room in the first carriage.

### Accommodation

- **Strenči:** first-night stay; the evening became a substantial social gathering and affected the next morning's condition.
- **Cēsis:** **Ozolkalns Camping**. The recorded shared accommodation cost was **€25 per person**.
- **Sigulda:** overnight stop before the final day.

Camping gear, hammocks, sleeping bags and bicycle luggage were discussed extensively before departure. The tour therefore involved more carried equipment than the group's later hotel-based expeditions.

### Return transport

The final riders finished in Carnikava and returned home from there. The chat again notes poor bicycle accommodation on the newer trains.

**Planning lesson:** for groups travelling by train with many bicycles, bicycle capacity must be checked early and a fallback boarding strategy should be prepared.

### Ride character

The first stage started late because of the train journey. Conditions alternated between light rain and dry periods. The route was mostly rideable roads—gravel and asphalt—with some off-road elements.

- Edge of the Seda bog area
- Timely arrival in Strenči
- Mikus joining the evening by motorcycle

### Planning insight

A **50–55 km first day** works well after a midday train arrival, especially when the group is carrying gear. Despite the modest distance, the day still occupied more than six hours because almost 45% of the elapsed time was spent stopped.

## Day 2 — Strenči → Valmiera → Cēsis

**Date:** 2 August 2024  
**Surface/type:** Gravel, asphalt and difficult wet MTB sections  
**Distance:** **70.3 km**  
**Elevation gain (Strava):** **513 m**  
**Start:** **10:49**  
**Finish:** **20:00**  
**Elapsed time:** **9 h 12 min**  
**Estimated moving time:** **4 h 21 min**  
**Estimated stopped time:** **4 h 50 min**  
**Estimated moving average:** **16.2 km/h**

The GPX indicates approximately **eleven pauses longer than five minutes**, totalling around **4 h 47 min**. Two major pauses lasted slightly more than one hour each.

### Ride character

The morning started heavily after a successful first-night party. The social atmosphere continued with a stop in **Brenguļi**.

From Valmiera onward, the route became wet, technical and physically demanding. The group followed difficult MTB terrain rather than simple connecting roads.

### Serious incident

Toms Romanovskis crashed and suffered a head injury. He was transported to hospital for examination and CT results. The hospital assessment found no concussion, but he still had head pain the following day and stopped the expedition.

The chat shows strong group support:

- Artis helped with transport so Toms could return to the group for the evening.
- The group adjusted logistics around the hospital visit.
- Toms was later able to leave hospital, but correctly decided not to continue riding.

### Planning insight

This day demonstrates that **70 km of wet MTB can be much harder than 90 km of ordinary gravel or asphalt**. Terrain type, technical difficulty and weather must be weighted more heavily than distance when assessing a social group's daily load.

The incident also reinforces the importance of:

- mandatory helmets;
- an emergency transport plan;
- keeping the group connected;
- avoiding overly technical trails when riders have mixed skill levels;
- preserving an easier alternative route.

## Day 3 — Cēsis → Līgatne → Sigulda

**Date:** 3 August 2024  
**Surface/type:** Predominantly MTB trails and off-road  
**Distance:** **53.7 km**  
**Elevation gain (Strava):** **633 m**  
**Start:** **11:27**  
**Finish:** **20:59**  
**Elapsed time:** **9 h 32 min**  
**Estimated moving time:** **3 h 57 min**  
**Estimated stopped time:** **5 h 35 min**  
**Estimated moving average:** **13.8 km/h**

The GPX indicates approximately **fourteen pauses longer than five minutes**. Total long-pause time was roughly **5 h 52 min**, including one stop of about **1 h 40 min**.

### Ride character

The group slept in beds at Ozolkalns Camping and began late. The day was physically exhausting despite being only about 54 km.

The stage included:

- technical MTB trails;
- off-road sections;
- repeated regrouping;
- nature stops;
- food and photo breaks;
- mechanical attention, including a tyre needing inflation;
- another fall involving Toms Ziemelis, who escaped with scratches.

The day was remembered for both **many laughs and many complaints**. It offered excellent Gauja National Park scenery, but extracted a high physical and mental price.

### Planning insight

This was the clearest example that **distance alone is a poor planning metric**. The group's average moving speed fell below 14 km/h, and more than half the entire day was spent stopped.

For future mixed groups:

- technical GNP MTB stages should be shortened to roughly **35–45 km**;
- an easier gravel/asphalt bypass should be available;
- start earlier if the route is expected to take nine or more hours;
- avoid placing another hard MTB day immediately after a serious incident and a long previous stage.

## Day 4 — Sigulda → Saulkrasti → Carnikava

**Date:** 4 August 2024  
**Surface/type:** MTB along the Gauja, then faster gravel/asphalt toward the coast  
**Distance:** **69.7 km**  
**Elevation gain (Strava):** **390 m**  
**Start:** **11:26**  
**Finish:** **17:51**  
**Elapsed time:** **6 h 25 min**  
**Estimated moving time:** **3 h 46 min**  
**Estimated stopped time:** **2 h 39 min**  
**Estimated moving average:** **18.6 km/h**

The GPX indicates approximately **nine pauses longer than five minutes**, totalling about **2 h 58 min**. The longest pause was roughly **57 minutes**.

### Ride character

The final day began late and without urgency. It initially followed MTB terrain along the Gauja toward Murjāņi, including some backtracking. The latter part became faster as the remaining three riders pushed toward Saulkrasti and Carnikava.

The chat records a separate **27 km Saulkrasti–Carnikava** section and confirms that all remaining riders reached home that evening.

### Planning insight

The final day worked because:

- the group had reduced to three stronger and motivated riders;
- the route became less technical later in the day;
- the finish had convenient rail access;
- the moving average increased substantially compared with Day 3.

However, a 70 km final day would not have been suitable for the original full mixed group after the preceding two difficult MTB stages.

## Before departure

- Six people initially voted that they expected to participate in the full tour, while one planned to finish earlier.
- Most planned to use bicycle bags; a smaller number considered backpacks.
- The group discussed tents, hammocks, sleeping mats, carrying water and bicycle packing setups.
- One participant withdrew shortly before the tour because of imminent family commitments.
- Toms Romanovskis had a last-minute crank and bottom-bracket problem, reinstalled his old crank and still joined the tour.
- The precise route was still being adjusted immediately before departure, particularly the Valmiera–Cēsis section.

- Train bicycle capacity was already a problem on departure.
- Rain and wet terrain increased the difficulty.
- The first evening became a large social event, creating a difficult start to Day 2.
- Brenguļi became another social stop during Day 2.

- Artis coordinated transport so Toms could return to the group for the evening.

Jānis summarized the expedition as approximately 200 km, mostly on MTB routes in Latvia's “outdoor mecca,” with punctures, falls, rain and exhaustion—but ultimately a successful adventure.

Toms Ģērmanis described it as both a serious challenge and a great experience, emphasizing the need to train, use helmets and improve cycling equipment before the next expedition.

The day after finishing, participants were already saying they wanted to ride again. This strongly supports the group's definition of a successful expedition.

# Lessons for Future Expeditions

## What worked well

- Gauja National Park delivered exceptional natural scenery and memorable riding.
- The route connected several strong destinations: Strenči, Valmiera, Cēsis, Līgatne, Sigulda and the coast.
- Flexible participation allowed riders to join or leave according to availability.
- The group reacted well to an emergency and supported the injured rider.
- Social evenings, communal food and shared difficulties created a strong expedition atmosphere.
- A rail-accessible finish simplified the final return.
- Despite the difficulty, participants quickly wanted another cycling expedition.

## What was too hard

- Two consecutive long technical MTB days were excessive for a mixed social group.
- Wet trails dramatically increased risk, fatigue and elapsed time.
- A late start after a heavy first evening reduced the margin for problems.
- Camping luggage added weight and complexity.
- The route had insufficient separation between “main route” and “easier fallback route.”
- The full expedition was only completed by three riders.

## Safety lessons

- Helmets should be treated as mandatory.
- A head impact ends the riding day and requires medical evaluation.
- Each day should have an emergency exit point and accessible road or station.
- Technical terrain should match the least technically confident rider, not the strongest rider.
- After rain, difficult MTB sections should be replaceable with gravel or asphalt alternatives.
- A first-aid kit, charged phones, shared live locations and emergency contact plan are essential.

## Logistics lessons

- Confirm train bicycle capacity and boarding strategy before departure.
- Do not finalize technically complex routes on the night before departure.
- For future tours, hotels or guest houses are preferable to carrying camping equipment.
- Plan accommodation first when availability is limited, then build the route around it.
- A shorter final day is preferable unless only the strongest riders remain.

## Route-design lessons

- **50–55 km** is suitable for a late-arrival first day.
- **70 km wet MTB** can consume an entire nine-hour day.
- **50–55 km technical GNP MTB** can be harder than an 80–90 km gravel stage.
- For mixed groups, technical stages should generally stay near **35–45 km**.
- A normal full day on good gravel/asphalt can remain around **70–90 km**.
- The itinerary should identify each stage by technical difficulty, not merely distance and elevation.

# Influence on Future Planning

The 2024 Gauja National Park expedition established several important principles for later tours:

- Beautiful terrain alone does not make a route suitable for a mixed group.
- Surface, weather and technical difficulty matter more than kilometres.
- A memorable tour should remain challenging, but it should not depend on repeated exhaustion, crashes or group fragmentation.
- National parks remain excellent destinations, but their MTB routes require conservative stage design.
- Easier alternatives must be planned before departure, not improvised after the group is already tired.
- Accommodation without camping equipment will make future tours lighter, simpler and more social.
- Train logistics must be treated as a core part of route design.
- The ideal future expedition keeps the scenery and social atmosphere of this tour while removing unnecessary technical risk.

---

## Overall Assessment

The 2024 Gauja National Park expedition was **beautiful, memorable and successful—but harder and riskier than necessary**.

It showed the group's resilience, humour and ability to support one another under pressure. It also provided the clearest evidence that future social tours should favour good gravel, quiet asphalt and manageable forest roads over prolonged technical MTB routes.

This expedition should be remembered not as a model to repeat exactly, but as a valuable benchmark: preserve the adventure, scenery and group spirit while designing a safer and more inclusive route.

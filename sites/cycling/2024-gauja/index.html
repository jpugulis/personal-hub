Gauja was the most beautiful route the group has ridden and the one it should be most careful about repeating.

Seven riders started. Three finished. The attrition was not caused by distance — no day exceeded 71 kilometres — but by the combination of wet technical mountain-bike terrain, four consecutive days, camping equipment on the bikes, and a serious crash.

Four days in the rain

Day 1 was a late start out of Valka after the train, alternating rain and dry spells across gravel and asphalt, past the Kaņepju oak and the edge of the Seda bog to a timely finish in Strenči — where Mikus arrived by motorcycle to join the evening. The evening became a substantial party, which shaped everything that followed.

Day 2 began heavily, continued socially through Brenguļi, and then turned difficult. From Valmiera onward the route followed wet, technical MTB terrain rather than connecting roads. Toms Romanovskis crashed, suffered a head injury and was taken to hospital for a CT scan. It found no concussion, but he still had head pain the next day and correctly stopped riding. Artis helped arrange transport so he could rejoin the group that evening.

Day 3 was the hardest day in the archive by feel and by data: 53.7 km that took 9 hours 32 minutes, an average moving speed of 13.8 km/h, and more than five and a half hours spent stopped. It included a puncture, another fall — Toms Ziemelis, scratches only — and, in the group's own description, many laughs and many complaints. It also delivered the finest scenery of the tour.

By Day 4 the group was three. The remaining riders followed the Gauja to Murjāņi, ate burgers at Raganas 66, took the road through Sēja and finished with a fast gravel run from Saulkrasti to Carnikava.

The lesson the archive kept re-learning

Gauja is the clearest demonstration that distance is a poor planning metric. Fifty-four kilometres of wet Gauja singletrack consumed an entire day and left the group exhausted. Two years later, 106 kilometres of flat dry Kurzeme coast took under two hours longer and produced the lowest heart rates ever recorded on a tour.

2024 Day 3 — 53.7 km, 9 h 32 min, 13.8 km/h.
2026 Day 2 — 106.2 km, 11 h 28 min, 19.6 km/h.

The gradient data, once reconciled, adds a nuance. At 7.4 m/km Gauja is genuinely hilly — second only to Latgale — but it is not the climbing that broke the group. It was surface, moisture and consecutive days.

What changed afterwards

Almost everything about how the group plans now traces back to this tour. Helmets became non-negotiable. Every stage since has had an emergency exit and a road or station within reach. Technical terrain is now matched to the least confident rider rather than the strongest. Camping equipment was dropped in favour of guest houses. And the group stopped treating a national park's MTB network as automatically suitable for a mixed social group.

The remarkable thing is what did not change: the day after finishing, the riders who had left early were already asking about the next one. By the group's own definition — everyone wanting to plan the next trip before this one has finished — Gauja was a success. It was simply a more expensive one than it needed to be.

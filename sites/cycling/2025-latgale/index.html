Latgale was the expedition that got the group's route philosophy right and its arithmetic wrong.

The riding was excellent. The group had agreed in advance to prioritise scenic gravel, avoid highways, avoid muddy singletrack, and keep every road rideable on mixed bicycle types — and Latgale delivered on all four. Daugavas Loki gave the longest day, Rāznas National Park the most varied, and the short final stage into Rēzekne set a template the group has used ever since.

It also produced the archive's first deliberate pre-ride preparation: Toms Ziemelis led morning yoga on two of the three mornings, running the group through Tadej Pogačar's activation routine before anyone got on a bike.

The hilliest ground the group has ridden

At 9.6 metres of climbing per kilometre, Latgale is steeper than anything else in the archive — more than double the Kurzeme coast at 4.7, and well clear of Gauja at 7.4. This is worth stating plainly, because Gauja has the reputation as the hard one. It was hard, but not because of gradient; it was hard because of wet technical trails and four consecutive days.

The steepest single day of all was the shortest: Day 3 into Rēzekne packed 430 metres into 36.5 kilometres, 11.8 m/km. The ride notes called it "kalnaina granteņu diena" — a hilly gravel day — and the numbers agree.

The correction

In August 2026, while building this site, all four expedition reports were checked against their Strava source data. The 2025 report did not survive the check.

Every ride time in it was three hours early. The figures had been transcribed from raw GPX timestamps in UTC without converting to Latvian local time. Day 1 was recorded as starting at 08:19; it started at 11:19. Day 1 was recorded as finishing at 17:36; it finished at 20:36.

The original report concluded: "The GPX data suggests that the group naturally settles into a comfortable expedition rhythm: start riding around 08:00." The group has never started a tour day at 08:00 — in any year.

That false principle then sat in the planning documents for a year, quietly shaping expectations about what a day would look like. The real pattern across four expeditions is an average start of 11:46 in 2023, 11:55 in 2024, 11:09 in 2025 and 10:19 in 2026. The group starts around eleven.

This matters practically. A day that begins at 11:00 and includes four hours of stopping finishes after 20:00 — as Latgale's first two days did, at 20:36 and 19:42. Any stage that has to meet a train needs to be planned from a realistic 11:00 start, or the early start has to be explicitly agreed the evening before. That second option is precisely what made the 2026 Kurzeme long day work.

Elevation was reconciled at the same time; the original figures ran about 17% high, and the total came down from 2,406 m to 2,056 m.

What stands

The route conclusions survived the correction intact. National parks are excellent destinations. Good gravel beats busy asphalt. Seventy to ninety kilometres a day suits a mixed group. Scenic stops are part of the day rather than interruptions to it. And a short final day timed to transport is the right way to end a tour — a principle Kurzeme adopted directly a year later and executed to the minute.

One new lesson emerged only from the correction itself: an expedition archive is only as useful as it is accurate. A single unconverted timezone produced a confident, specific and entirely false planning principle that went unchallenged for a year.

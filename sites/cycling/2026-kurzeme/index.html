Peak heat

Kurzeme is flat, and that turned out to be the whole story. Four years of expeditions had been quietly teaching the group that distance is not what makes a day hard — surface, gradient and weather are. In 2026 the group finally rode a route where none of those three were working against it, and the result was the biggest three-day total it has ever covered, at the lowest heart rates it has ever recorded.

Six riders started: Jānis Pūgulis organising, Toms Ģērmanis, Katrīna, Toms Ziemelis, Artūrs Cekuliņš and Tomāts. Two were on their first expedition. Half the group was on gravel bikes and half on mountain bikes, one of them an older frame on 26" wheels. All six finished the full route — the first time that has happened.

The coast

The tour opened the night before it began, with a 5.5 km ride from Liepāja station to a campsite at Beberliņi lake in the dark. The train had been packed with Summer Sound festival traffic and police were working the carriages with drug dogs; arriving at a lake instead of a hotel lobby reset the mood entirely.

Day 1 ran north up the coast. The group passed the Karosta forts without stopping — a decision that mattered more than it sounds, because the stopping time it saved went into Pāvilosta instead. There the group swam and then ate in the garden behind Café Laiva, and stayed for the best part of two hours out of a seven-hour day. Further north at Ulmale they stopped again for the big-wave surf spot along the cliffs, a place that had been a curiosity during planning and became a genuine stop on the route.

Sixty-eight kilometres, 109 metres of climbing, an average heart rate of 124, and 55% of the day spent standing still. That is the shape of day the group had been trying to design since 2023.

An evening supply run to the Jūrkalne shop and back — 11 km with the bags left at camp, ridden at 24.4 km/h with sprint stages. The fastest average speed of the whole expedition, and it was for fun.

The long day

Day 2 was 106 kilometres, the longest day the group has ever ridden together. It was planned that way. The tour had to reach the Tukums railway line, and Jānis did not want more than 60–70 km left for the final day, which meant finding a bed somewhere after Renda and before Jaunmokas. Sabile fit exactly.

What made it work was that everybody knew. The long day was flagged from the first evening, the 09:05 start was agreed in advance — the earliest departure in the entire four-year archive — and nobody was ambushed by it.

The day held a 40-minute breakfast at Virši in Jūrkalne, and then one of the quietest moments of the tour: nearly forty minutes on a small boardwalk at Ēdole, sitting among swans doing nothing in particular. After Kuldīga came two Mežtaka forest sections, Kuldīga–Renda and Renda–Sabile, both comfortably rideable on every bike in the group. The riders reached Sabile at 20:34 and swam in the Abava, beating the rain by minutes. That evening the town was shut and silent under the downpour.

The numbers behind the headline are the interesting part. Average heart rate on the longest day was 114 bpm — the lowest of the tour. Only 5 hours 24 minutes of the 11 hours 28 were spent moving. At 4.6 metres of climbing per kilometre, it was less than half as steep as an average Latgale day.

Kuldīga

The most instructive thing that happened on this tour happened sitting down, in a restaurant, at kilometre 47.

The Sabile accommodation had deliberately been left unbooked. The plan was to reach Kuldīga around the halfway point and only then decide how far the group could realistically get. By the time everyone was eating at Pagrabiņš the mood had split — Katrīna was low, and Tomāts was seriously weighing a train back to Liepāja for the Summer Sound party. The 69-minute lunch stop in the data was not simply a long meal.

What is striking is that the anxiety was about the route, not the bed. The question was not where will we sleep but how far can we actually get.

It was solved by the group rather than by the organiser. Artūrs and Katrīna pulled up option after option for Jānis to check until they circled back to one they had already seen — Viesu māja "Rambules" in the centre of Sabile. The host had stepped back from the guest-house business and had made clear he had no interest in parties, boat groups or young people looking for a night out. Jānis called and made a deliberate effort to be very polite. The host agreed: he would take them, if they were six people.

They were six. Rambules — a large terrace, a beautifully kept garden full of flowers, a courteous host — turned out to be the best accommodation of the tour. And handing two struggling riders a problem to solve did more for the group than any amount of encouragement would have.

Kandava

The best thirty minutes of the expedition were not on a bicycle.

Day 3 began before the bikes did: at 07:55 Jānis ran 5 km around Sabile — the puppet park, Vīna kalns, the town centre — and finished with a swim in the Abava. The group's own start was slower, with a shop visit and a geocache hunt eating 18 minutes before anyone had covered half a kilometre.

The Sabile–Jaunmokas Mežtaka section was hilly and heavily gravel, but rideable throughout, and somewhere along it the group fell into a cheerful conversation with a party of German tourists. Then lunch landed next to the Kandava sports centre, where the big water jumps are.

Rock–paper–scissors decided the allocation. Jānis won immediately and took the 5-metre board. Toms Ģērmanis drew 7 metres. Toms Ziemelis drew the 10-metre platform. All three climbed up, and all three jumped: Ziemis with an enormous double-footed entry, Tomy with a running jump, Jānis flappy-bird style. Going up was, in his words, scary as hell. Nobody backed out.

In 37 °C the second Mežtaka section, Jaunmokas–Tukums, was substantially shortened — the single best tactical decision of the tour, protecting both the train and the group's mood. They reached Tukums station at 18:00 exactly as planned, and rode home from the Riga end that evening.

What it proved

Compare Day 2 with 2024: Gauja's third day was 54.3 km and took 9 hours 32 minutes at 13.8 km/h. Kurzeme's second day was almost twice the distance and took under two hours longer. Terrain and surface decide what a day costs; distance is a poor third.

The lesson to carry forward is not ride 106 km days. It is that when the terrain is chosen honestly for the group that is actually riding, the group turns out to be capable of far more than the plan assumed — and has far more fun doing less of the riding.
